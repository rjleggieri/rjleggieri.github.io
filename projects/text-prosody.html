Exploratory graduate research · The University of Texas at Austin

Comparing text sentiment and vocal affect

An exploratory comparison of text-derived sentiment and audio-derived prosody in a shared valence-arousal representation.

Question and reported results

I designed and evaluated the Alignment Discrepancy Score (ADS) using 189 DAIC-WOZ interviews, utterance-level alignment, and five-fold participant-level cross-validation. Reported AUC was 0.62 for sentiment-ADS, 0.49 for audio alone, and 0.70 for text alone.

What the comparison showed

The discrepancy score did not outperform the text-only baseline. PHQ-elevated sessions showed a tighter score distribution with fewer high-score outliers. This is an exploratory observation; it does not establish a psychological mechanism or clinical validity.

The strongest lesson was the need to scrutinize construct validity, calibration, grouping, and the evidence required to interpret a difference between model outputs. The score is not a diagnostic tool or a measure of internal emotional state.

Separate engineering follow-on

text-prosody-evidence turns those evaluation questions into a dataset-neutral reference framework. Its current evidence is explicitly bounded and does not validate the original course-study results.
