Evaluating NLP robustness through training-data analysis

A bounded experiment in DeBERTa training and evaluation: use training dynamics to inform category-weighted loss, then inspect the trade-off between MNLI and HANS performance.

Read revised paperOriginal course report

Question and implementation

Could training-data weighting improve challenge-set behavior without a large loss in standard validation accuracy? I applied dataset cartography to 392,702 MNLI training examples over five epochs. A custom Hugging Face callback captured per-example logits and gold labels; a weighted PyTorch dataset and Trainer override incorporated category weights into cross-entropy loss.

Reported results

Original experiment; results preserved in the revised paper
EvaluationBaselineRe-weightedChange
HANS accuracy77.49%78.59%+1.10 pp
MNLI accuracy88.91%88.47%−0.44 pp

The selected weights were 0.5 for easy examples, 1.0 for ambiguous examples, and 0.5 for hard examples, with a variability threshold of 0.2. The gain is modest and uneven across HANS subcases; several challenging constructions remain largely unsolved.

Interpretation and limits

The result supports further investigation of training-data weighting. It does not establish general removal of linguistic shortcuts, improved fairness, or statistical significance. Worse performance after increasing hard-example weights raises questions about noise, ambiguity, model limitations, and optimization; it does not prove label errors.

The original report does not establish repeated-seed variability or a separate held-out challenge set for configuration selection. The revised discussion makes that uncertainty explicit, preserves all result tables, corrects verifiable references, and retains the original bibliography in an appendix. No model training was rerun for the editorial revision.

What this work demonstrates

Training instrumentation, transformer fine-tuning, custom loss implementation, comparison across evaluation distributions, and communicating both useful results and unresolved questions. Course implementation remains private; the public paper documents the method and reported evidence.
